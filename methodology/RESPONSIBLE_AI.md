# Methodology and Responsible AI

## Purpose

This portfolio evaluates workflows, not people. It demonstrates controlled prompt design, rubric calibration, annotation quality, operational analysis and documented decision-making.

## Data provenance

- All ticket, response, workforce and incident records are synthetic.
- Names, emails, phone numbers and account details are represented only by placeholders.
- No confidential employer records, production prompts or client instructions are included.
- Randomized operational values use a fixed seed so results can be regenerated.

## Human oversight

Automated checks can identify schema failures, mismatched labels and KPI exceptions. They cannot determine whether every business decision is appropriate. Human review is required for:

- ambiguous or multi-intent records;
- security and privacy escalations;
- policy exceptions;
- cultural or locale-specific interpretation;
- changes to label definitions or acceptance thresholds.

## Evaluation discipline

- Criteria are written before outcomes are scored.
- Accuracy is separated from schema compliance and safety behavior.
- Agreement statistics are reported with sample sizes and limitations.
- Perfect results are not treated as automatically credible.
- Failure cases remain visible so improvements can be audited.

## Bias and limitations

The datasets are deliberately small and designed for demonstration. They cannot establish production performance, model generalization or fairness across populations. Network-support examples reflect Jesse Tabler's documented technical domain and should not be generalized to unrelated high-stakes domains such as medicine, law or lending.

## Change control

Material revisions should update the project version, regenerate the validation summary and record the change in `CHANGELOG.md`.
